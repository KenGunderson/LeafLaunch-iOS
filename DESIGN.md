# LeafLaunch customer storefront

## Overview

LeafLaunch is a native SwiftUI commerce app for the business assigned to its target. The design makes products, real prices, saved favorites, and store information easy to browse. Its catalog uses stacked category and subcategory rails with moving accent underlines, native filtering, and a shared image-first product grid.

The skin is a construction system, not a fixed brand. Each store owns its appearance through Rune. Preserve the store's configured colors, fonts, radius roles, logo, and existing theme persistence. Product familiarity and clear actions take priority over decorative layouts.

## Colors

Rune is the only runtime styling authority. Production views read `@Environment(\.style)`; semantic Rune color sources and modifiers resolve that same environment. Outside the explicit promotion-card and membership-card treatments below, do not introduce feature palettes, literal colors, gradients, `Style.shared` reads, or a second theme engine. Pass the environment style explicitly when an API such as `source(style:)` or `resolvedColor(style:)` requires color resolution.

The mapping remains:

`StoreAppBranding -> validated cached definition / StoreThemeBuilder -> Rune Theme -> environment style -> shared components`

[LeafLaunchLightTheme](LeafLaunch/Services/DesignSystem/LeafLaunchLightTheme.swift), [LeafLaunchDarkTheme](LeafLaunch/Services/DesignSystem/LeafLaunchDarkTheme.swift), [LeafLaunchThemeTokens](LeafLaunch/Services/DesignSystem/LeafLaunchThemeTokens.swift), and [LeafLaunchFonts](LeafLaunch/Services/DesignSystem/LeafLaunchFonts.swift) remain canonical fallback sources. This document defines composition, without duplicating their values. Cached branding restores synchronously before splash creation; the supported remote radius and font contracts remain intact.

Promotion cards use the active Rune palette.link darkened by a 78-percent black overlay, with white main copy. The View Offer button uses the unmodified accent and text.titleInverted, which the store theme derives for contrast against the accent. This treatment stays local to StorePromotionCard and remains dark in both themes. Store Info inherits the themed page canvas; its action icons and current-day highlight use a faint accent tint, matching the age tile and location selection.

Use background.primary for the canvas, secondary for content surfaces, tertiary/quinary for media placeholders and quiet grouping, and border for outlines. Title/body roles carry content hierarchy. Foreground.primary pairs with text.button for filled selection and primary actions; accent/link, icon, and button roles stay independent. Semantic error and destructive roles retain their meaning. The same construction must work in light, dark, and store-defined themes.

## Typography

Use Rune `appFont(with:)` and the active store's font roles. Section titles use TITLE_6, branded navigation headers use HEADER_3 (Account and Points Activity use HEADER_5, while Menu has a separate BILLBOARD_2 display title to match the catalog reference), product card names use TITLE_3, and card prices use TITLE_5. Product details use TITLE_7 for the standalone product name, BODY_3 for the category/subcategory line, HEADER_2 for Size & Pricing, and TITLE_6 for variant prices. Supporting copy uses BODY_1/BODY_2 or NOTE_1; compact event dates/schedules use the 12-point NOTE_2 role while retaining the configured store font. Do not set a new font family or fixed custom type scale in a feature.

App-owned titles, section headings, field labels, and calls to action use Title Case, capitalizing every word, including A, And, At, For, Of, Or, The, and To. Examples include Featured Products, View Menu, Store Hours, Holiday Hours, View Offer, See All, Phone Number, Email The Store, Good To See You, Your Collection, Product Details, Size & Pricing, and Questions About This Product?. Keep descriptive body copy in sentence case and preserve the supplied spelling and casing of store, product, event, and promotion names.

Let descriptive text wrap naturally outside product cards. Product cards reserve one category line and two name lines, keep price/availability on one line, and use a fixed details area with bottom-aligned pricing so their heights stay equal. Product grids retain two equal-width columns. Keep native control behavior; app-owned components, views, and screens do not add accessibility modifiers or read `accessibilityReduceMotion`.

## Layout

Horizontal storefront rails use small gaps to match the reference. Forms retain medium field gaps; media dimensions, hero offsets, safe areas, tab clearance, and the six-point promotion-dot gap are intentional geometry exceptions. The grid uses eight-point gaps in both directions. Category/name copy is top-aligned, and an infinity-height frame anchors the shared twenty-four-point price slot at the bottom. Unavailable products show Sold out in the price position; available products with missing prices keep Price Unavailable. ## Elevation & Depth

Use `LLAppCardView` for app-owned card composition. It wraps Rune `AppCardView` with shadow disabled and applies Rune `.appBorder` with a 0.8-point line width and its default color. The wrapper owns these defaults; spacing, background, and corner radius remain configurable Rune roles. `CommerceSurface` supplies the recurring padded surface with theme large radius. Promotion and event cards use unpadded `LLAppCardView` composition. Do not nest cards just to divide text; prefer spacing or a disclosure within the owning surface.

## Shapes

Surfaces, Settings cards, and media use theme large radius; product cards, product-detail gallery/table/contact surfaces, and inputs use medium2, and primary actions retain LeafLaunchButton's theme medium radius; its prominent footer size uses medium2 radius. Category tabs stay flat with an accent underline; sort controls remain capsules. The tab bar keeps its documented shape. All other radius choices come from the active Rune theme.

Native SwiftUI owns scrolling, focus, menus, pickers, disclosures, and safe areas. Rune `ImageSource` owns media loading and caching. Public product responses include each active image’s `url`; cards and galleries load those URLs directly. Categories provide `image_url`, bootstrap branding provides `logo_url`/`icon_url`/`splash_url`, and promotion/event images provide `url`. Customer models contain no storage keys or MIME metadata, and screens make no separate media URL requests. Keep loading, unavailable-media, empty, and failure/retry states truthful.

Modal information uses its natural height when it fits and scrolls when needed, with the primary dismissal action outside the scrolling content. Use themed presentation spacing rather than a fixed gap that can consume the available height on short screens.

Apply `.animation(.bouncy, value:)` to meaningful selection, favorite feedback, disclosures, and splash entrance. Category and subcategory selection animate their matched-geometry underlines; changing the parent selects its first child, and the child group reveals/hides through blur replacement. Category headings and sort labels use animated numeric text content transitions. Favorite actions show ProductFavoriteToastView through Rune's custom-content toast initializer, naming the product and whether it was added or removed. The toast view spans the current screen width minus 32 points, leaving sixteen-point side insets. Use Rune `.asButton` for ordinary custom actions; retain native role-bearing buttons where the modifier cannot preserve that role. Keep LeafLaunchButton and Rune CommonButton for primary action styling. Do not add perpetual decorative animation or delays to the startup callback.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Keep store branding and all visual tokens in Rune; improve hierarchy through construction.
- Use actual model imagery, prices, schedules, availability, balances, and ledger strings.
- Keep icon actions at least 44pt touch targets.
- Keep search, filtering, server sorting, pagination, local favorites, legal copy, and lifecycle ownership consistent with [UX-CONTRACT](UX-CONTRACT.md).
- Check changed call sites and both app targets. Follow the repository's restriction on Xcode builds and tests.
